01

A Decade of Rising Numbers

National traffic hospitalisations climbed steadily from 2011 to 2019, before a brief COVID-related dip in 2020. By 2021, numbers had nearly recovered — suggesting structural factors beyond pandemic effects.

⚠ Data note: These are absolute counts. Population growth over this period means per-capita rates may tell a different story.

Who Gets Hurt Most?

Car drivers account for the largest share of hospitalisations — but motorcyclists and pedal cyclists carry a disproportionate burden relative to their share of road users. Select a road user type to highlight their trend.

03

Age & the Road

The 40–64 age group consistently records the highest hospitalisations — challenging the assumption that road injuries are predominantly a young person's problem. Hover over bars to explore each age group across years.

04

State by State

NSW and Victoria dominate in raw counts — reflecting their larger populations. The Northern Territory's smaller numbers mask a significantly higher per-capita risk given its population of under 300,000.

⚠ Data note: Direct state comparisons using raw counts are misleading. NSW has ~8× the population of the NT. Per-capita rates would significantly alter this picture.

05

A Growing Disparity

First Nations hospitalisations nearly doubled between 2011 and 2021 — growing at a rate far exceeding the non-Indigenous population. Given that First Nations peoples make up approximately 3.2% of the Australian population, this disparity is stark.

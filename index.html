A Decade in Numbers

National road crash hospitalisations climbed through the 2010s, with a COVID-related dip in 2020. Car occupants dominate in absolute volume; motorcyclists carry a disproportionate share relative to their road presence.

Who Gets Hurt?

Working-age adults (26–64) account for the majority of hospitalisations. Males outnumber females in nearly every road user category. The heatmap reveals which age–road user combinations drive the most harm.

03

Where It Happens

The Northern Territory records the highest hospitalisation rate per capita — over four times the national average. First Nations Australians experienced a near-doubling of hospitalisations while non-Indigenous figures grew ~15%. Remote communities bear a disproportionate burden.

How It Happens

Collisions with other vehicles dominate for most road users. Fixed objects are the leading counterparty for motorcyclists. The Sankey diagram traces the road user → counterparty flow; bar chart shows how counterparty patterns shifted over time.

⚠ "Other/unspecified" counterparty category may be large in some states — interpret with caution.

05

Key Findings

Volume trending up

National hospitalisations rose through 2019, dipped during COVID-19 mobility restrictions in 2020, then recovered. Structural factors — not just pandemic effects — drive long-term risk.

Disparity widening

First Nations hospitalisations nearly doubled over the decade while Non-Indigenous figures grew ~15%. Remote and regional communities are disproportionately affected.

NT rate stands out

The Northern Territory's per-capita hospitalisation rate far exceeds every other state — a product of higher road speeds, remoteness from hospitals, and demographic factors.

Motorcyclists over-represented

Despite a small share of vehicle kilometres travelled, motorcyclists account for a consistently high share of serious hospitalisations — primarily from fixed-object collisions.
